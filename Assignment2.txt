Imagine you're trying to tell a friend a story, but you leave the ending out. They ask you what happens next, and you don't
know. So, you give them some clues to help them guess. In a Large Language Model (LLM), the decoder is like a helper that takes
these clues (or tokens) and uses them to make an educated guess about what the next token should be.

Think of the decoder as a "story completion machine". It receives a sequence of tokens (like words) from the input, and it
tries to figure out what the next token should be to make a coherent story. The decoder uses its knowledge of language patterns
and relationships to make a prediction, and then generates the next token. This process is repeated until the entire story is
completed.

In technical terms, the decoder processes tokens one by one, using a neural network to predict the next token based on the

decoder's goal is to produce a coherent and meaningful output that makes sense in the context of the conversation or story.

Output:
The decoder is a critical component of a Large Language Model (LLM), responsible for generating the next token in a sequence to
complete a sentence, paragraph, or document. Its primary role is to perform next-token prediction, which involves predicting
the probability distribution over all possible next tokens given the input tokens. This is achieved through self-attention, a
mechanism that allows the decoder to weigh the importance of different input tokens when generating the next token.

The decoder also employs causal masking to prevent the model from considering future tokens when generating the next token.
Additionally, it incorporates positional information to maintain context and track the sequence of tokens. The output of the
decoder is further processed by a feed-forward network (FFN) and combined with the output of the encoder through a residual
connection, allowing the decoder to refine its predictions and improve the overall coherence of the generated output. The
decoder layers work together to progressively generate the next tokens, with each layer building upon the previous one to
create a more accurate and context-dependent output.

The decoder architecture can be thought of as a series of independent sub-modules, each responsible for a specific aspect of
next-token prediction. The multiple decoder layers work together to generate the output by iterating through the input sequence
and generating one token at a time. The output of each layer is fed into the next layer, allowing the decoder to refine its
predictions and generate a coherent and context-dependent output. This hierarchical structure enables the decoder to capture
long-range dependencies and relationships in the input sequence, resulting in more accurate and natural language generation.
